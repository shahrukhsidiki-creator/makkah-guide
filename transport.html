Trains & buses

Haramain high-speed rail, intercity coaches, and getting around Makkah itself.

About timings on this page Rail and coach schedules change seasonally and surge during Ramadan and Hajj. Nothing here is a live timetable — the figures are indicative, to help you plan. Always check and book on the official site before you travel. The buttons below go straight there.

🚄 Haramain High Speed Railway

The fast, comfortable, air-conditioned way between Makkah, Jeddah and Madinah. Trains run at up to 300 km/h, and for the Makkah–Madinah journey the train beats the road comfortably on both time and stress.

The airport station is the one most pilgrims underuse. Landing at Jeddah and taking the train straight to Makkah avoids the road entirely.

Practical tips for the train
  • Book ahead in season. During Ramadan and around Hajj, trains sell out days in advance. Outside those periods you can often buy on the day.
  • Arrive 30–45 minutes early. There are security checks and the stations are large.
  • Luggage. Generous allowances by airline standards, but there's a limit on pieces and size — check when booking. Zamzam containers have their own rules.
  • Ihram on the train is normal. If you're travelling Madinah → Makkah for Umrah, you enter Ihram at Dhul Hulayfah before boarding, not at the station.
  • Factor in the taxi at both ends. Neither Makkah nor Madinah station is walkable from the mosque. Budget about 15 minutes and a short fare each side — see the fare calculator.

🚌 Intercity coaches (SAPTCO)

SAPTCO is the national coach operator. It is the cheapest way between cities by a wide margin, and the slowest. If budget is the binding constraint, this is your option.

Makkah ⇄ Jeddah≈ 1 hr 30about SAR 25–35
Makkah ⇄ Madinah≈ 5–6 hrabout SAR 70–100
Makkah ⇄ Taif≈ 2 hrabout SAR 30–40
Makkah ⇄ Riyadh≈ 10–12 hrabout SAR 150–200

Coaches are air-conditioned and make prayer stops. Bring your own food and water — the stops are brief.

🚍 Getting around Makkah

🚌 City buses

Makkah has a growing public bus network linking the main residential districts — Aziziyah, Shisha, Rusaifah, Kudai — to the Haram area. Cheap, air-conditioned, and much better value than taxis for routine trips.

🅿️ Haram shuttle buses

Free and paid shuttles run between the big hotel districts and the Haram's outer gates, especially around prayer times. Most large hotels run their own — ask at reception before you pay for a taxi.

🚕 Taxis & ride apps

Plentiful, mostly negotiated rather than metered. Know the rough rate before you agree.

🚶 Walking

Honestly the best option within about 2 km of the Haram, and often faster than a car after prayers. Walkways are wide and well lit. Avoid midday in summer.
