The Hateem & Hijr Ismail

Praying inside it counts as praying inside the Kaaba. Here's how access actually works.

No website can tell you today's exact entry time — including this one Access to the Hijr is opened and closed by the General Presidency on the day, based on crowding, and the schedule shifts constantly. Anyone publishing a fixed timetable is showing you something out of date. What this page gives you instead: how the system works, so you know what you're looking at — plus a button that pulls today's announcements live.

📐 What it actually is

The Hateem is the low, curved, white marble wall on the north-western side of the Kaaba. The semicircular open space between that wall and the Kaaba itself is the Hijr Ismail.

When the Kaaba was rebuilt by the Quraysh before the Prophet ﷺ received revelation, they ran short of lawful funds and built on a smaller footprint, leaving this portion outside the walls and marking it with the low wall. So this area is part of the Kaaba — it just isn't under the roof.

Two consequences follow, and they are the only two things you really need to hold on to:

  • Praying inside the Hijr is praying inside the Kaaba. This is why people queue for it.
  • Your Tawaf must go around the outside of the wall. Cut through it and that circuit doesn't count, because you didn't go around the Kaaba.

🚦 How the entry system works

It is not a free-for-all, and it has not been for years. The pattern you will encounter:

  • Barriers and staff. The Hijr is fenced off with movable barriers. Staff open a gap, admit a group, let them pray, then clear the area and admit the next group.
  • Separate access for men and women. Men and women are not admitted at the same time. Women's slots are typically fewer and shorter, which is why women often wait considerably longer.
  • Opened in the quiet hours. Access tends to be opened when the mataf is least congested — commonly in the window after Fajr, and in the late night or small hours. It is usually closed around the five prayers, and closed entirely when the mataf is full.
  • Closed in peak seasons. During the Hajj period, the last ten nights of Ramadan, and other surge times, the Hijr is frequently shut to the public for days at a stretch. Plan as if you may not get in.
  • Staff instructions override everything. If an askari tells you to move, move. Arguing gets the whole queue closed.

👣 The steps to follow

  1. Pick your moment

    Go in the quietest window you can manage — after Fajr, or very late at night. Mid-afternoon on a Friday is the worst possible choice.

  2. Find the queue, not the gap

    Walk the perimeter of the mataf near the Hijr and look for the marshalled line. There will be one. Joining it is the fastest route in; approaching the barrier directly is the fastest route to being turned away.

  3. Ask an askari

    Point at the Hijr and say "Hijr Ismail?" — they will tell you if it's open, and when. Many speak Urdu, English or Indonesian. See the phrase list.

  4. Enter when waved in, and pray two rak'ah

    Two rak'ah of nafl is the normal practice. Keep it short — people behind you have been waiting as long as you did.

  5. Make dua, then leave promptly

    Make your dua, then move out when asked. There is no specific prescribed dua for the Hijr — ask for what you came to ask for, in any language.

  6. If you don't get in, let it go

    Praying in the Hijr is a virtue, not an obligation, and not part of Umrah. Your Umrah is complete and accepted without it. Missing it costs you nothing in terms of the rites.

⚠️ Mistakes and misunderstandings

Walking through the Hijr during Tawaf

The single most consequential error on this page. Crowds sometimes push people across the line near the wall. If it happens, that circuit is void — redo it. When the mataf is dense, stay a few metres out from the wall rather than hugging it.

Thinking the Hijr must be entered for Umrah to be valid

It must not. It is not one of the pillars or obligations of Umrah. Entering it is a desirable extra when access is granted.

Believing there is a special Hijr dua

There is no specific prescribed supplication for the Hijr. Pray two rak'ah and ask in your own words.

Climbing or sitting on the marble wall

Not permitted, and you will be removed. It is also slippery and people have been injured falling from it.

Pushing in when a women's slot is running

Men attempting to enter during a women's window is a routine cause of slots being shut down early. Wait your turn.

Paying someone who "guarantees" entry

Nobody can sell you access to the Hijr. Anyone offering to is taking your money for something that is free and that they do not control.

🤲 A practical suggestion

Write down what you want to ask for before you join the queue. Entry is brief, often rushed, and the single most common regret people report afterwards is standing in a place where dua is accepted and finding their mind had gone completely blank.
