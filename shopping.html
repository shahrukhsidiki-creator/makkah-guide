Dates, gifts & shopping

What things should cost, where to buy them, and how not to be overcharged.

The single most useful thing on this page The shops directly facing the Haram charge two to four times what the same dates cost ten minutes away. Nothing about the product is different. If you buy your dates at the first shop you see, you will overpay — reliably, every time.

📍 Where to buy

🏆 Kudai dates market

The wholesale-style dates market area south of the Haram. Big sacks, real variety, prices well below the tourist shops, and vendors who expect you to taste before buying. Best value in Makkah for anyone buying more than a token box.

🛒 Bin Dawood & Al Mansour supermarkets

Large supermarket chains throughout Makkah, several very close to the Haram. Fixed prices, clear labelling, no haggling, and dates that are properly stored. Slightly more than the market but far less than the tourist shops — and the easiest option if bargaining isn't for you.

🕌 Madinah — the real home of Ajwa

If your trip includes Madinah, buy your Ajwa there. The dates market near the Prophet's Mosque and the roadside farms on the way to Quba sell genuinely local Ajwa, often noticeably cheaper and fresher than the same thing in Makkah.

🏬 Malls near the Haram

Abraj Al Bait (the clock tower), Makkah Mall, Al Diyafa — convenient, air-conditioned, fixed prices, and the most expensive dates in the city. Fine for a last-minute gift box; a poor choice for buying in bulk.

🔍 Spotting fake Ajwa

Ajwa commands a large premium, so cheaper dark varieties get sold as Ajwa constantly. Things to check:

  • Size and shape. Real Ajwa is small, oval and fairly uniform. Large dark dates being sold as Ajwa usually aren't.
  • Colour. Deep brown-black, matte, with fine silvery lines running along the skin. Glossy and uniformly jet-black is a warning sign.
  • Taste. Mild, subtly sweet, almost raisin-like. Ajwa is not intensely sweet — if it tastes like sugar, it's probably Safawi or Khudri.
  • Texture. Soft and dry to the touch, not sticky or wet. Heavy stickiness often means syrup has been added.
  • Price. Genuine Ajwa at SAR 20 per kilo does not exist. If it's remarkably cheap, it isn't Ajwa.
  • Always taste first. Any honest vendor in Kudai or Madinah will hand you one before you buy. A refusal tells you what you need to know.
Worth saying plainly Safawi is a genuinely excellent date at a fraction of the price, and most people can't tell it from Ajwa in a mixed box. If you're buying for a large family, Safawi or Sukkari will go much further than a small quantity of Ajwa.

🤝 Bargaining

  • Markets and small shops: haggling is expected. Supermarkets and malls: it isn't — don't try.
  • Ask the price of one kilo first. Prices quoted per box are hard to compare.
  • Buying several kilos or mixed varieties gets you a real discount. Ask for it.
  • Counter at roughly 60% of the first quote and settle near the middle.
  • Cash gets better prices than card in the markets.
  • Walking away genuinely works. There are twenty more vendors selling the same thing.
  • Check the packing. Ask for vacuum-sealed or boxed if it's going in a suitcase — loose dates in a hot bag become one solid mass.

🎁 What else is worth bringing back

💧 Zamzam

Official sealed containers from the authorised distribution points. Airlines have specific rules about carrying it — check yours before buying, and never accept unsealed bottles from a street seller.

🌸 Attar & oud

Alcohol-free perfume oils. Quality and price vary enormously; smell before you buy and don't pay tourist-shop prices for a small vial.

📿 Misbaha

Prayer beads in everything from plastic to agarwood. Cheap and light — the easiest gift for a long list of people.

🧎 Prayer mats

Turkish-made ones are the better quality. Padded travel mats are genuinely useful rather than just decorative.

📖 Quran & books

Excellent print quality and very reasonable prices. Translations available in most languages.

🧴 Kohl, miswak, honey

Light, cheap, traditional. Sidr honey is a real premium product — and widely faked, so buy from a proper shop.

🧳 Getting it home

  • Dates are heavy. Five kilos disappears into your allowance faster than you expect — weigh your bag before the airport.
  • Buy a cheap extra holdall in Makkah rather than paying excess baggage at check-in. It will cost a fraction.
  • Zamzam is usually handled as a separate allowance, not part of your normal baggage — but the rule differs by airline. Confirm yours.
  • Some countries restrict importing food, seeds or plant products. Check your own customs rules before you load up.
