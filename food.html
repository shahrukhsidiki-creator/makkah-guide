🍽️ Eating well without spending much

Where the cheap food actually is
  • Walk away from the Haram. Prices roughly halve between the clock-tower food courts and the side streets ten minutes out. The same biryani can be SAR 30 in one place and SAR 12 in the other.
  • Mall food courts — convenient, air-conditioned, mid-priced, and good when you're with a group that wants different things.
  • Workers' restaurants in the residential districts (Aziziyah, Misfalah, Shisha) serve full meals for very little. Busy with local workers is the signal to look for.
  • Supermarkets — bread, laban, fruit, dates and cooked items from the hot counter. Easily the cheapest way to eat, and useful for keeping food in the room.
  • Share. Portions in desi and Arab restaurants are large. Two people can often eat from one mandi or one karahi.
Eating around prayer times

Shops and restaurants close for congregational prayers and reopen afterwards. The stretch right after Maghrib is the busiest of the day near the Haram. Either eat well before the adhan, or wait out the rush — trying to eat in the twenty minutes after a prayer means queues everywhere.

Staying well
  • Stomach upsets are the most common thing that ruins an Umrah trip. Prefer food that is cooked fresh and served hot.
  • Be careful with salads, cut fruit and anything that's been sitting under a heat lamp.
  • Drink far more water than feels necessary. Zamzam is free and everywhere inside the Haram — use it.
  • Carry oral rehydration sachets. They weigh nothing and are the difference between losing an afternoon and losing three days.
  • In summer, eat lighter during the day. Heavy meals plus heat plus walking is how people end up in the clinic.
Dietary needs

All meat in Saudi Arabia is halal, so that is one thing you don't need to think about.

Vegetarian and vegan travellers do need to plan: South Indian, Egyptian (koshari, ful, falafel) and Lebanese mezze are your reliable options. Gluten-free is harder — supermarkets in the larger malls are your best bet.

🛵 Delivery

Delivery works well in Makkah and is often cheaper than eating near the Haram — useful when you're exhausted after Tawaf and don't want to go back out.

Give the hotel name and a landmark rather than relying on the map pin — addressing near the Haram is difficult and riders often can't find the pin.
