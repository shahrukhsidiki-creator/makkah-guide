Ziyarat in Makkah

The sites worth your time, what they actually involve, and how to visit them properly.

Before the list — a word on intention None of these places is part of Umrah, and visiting them earns no specific prescribed reward. Visit them to see and reflect on where these events happened. Do not seek blessing from the stones, do not tie cloth to anything, do not leave notes in crevices, do not pray towards any of it, and don't pay someone to perform a ritual on your behalf. All of that has grown up around these sites and none of it is from the religion. Reflection is the point.

🗺️ Planning a ziyarat day

  • Most people cover the main Makkah sites in a half-day — around four to five hours.
  • Hire a car with a driver for the morning rather than taking separate taxis. Agree the full route and total price before you start, and confirm he waits at each stop.
  • Go early. By 9am in summer the heat makes Jabal al-Nour genuinely dangerous.
  • Take far more water than you think you need, a hat (not if you're in Ihram), and proper shoes.
  • Agency ziyarat tours cost more but handle the logistics and usually include a guide.

⛰️ The sites

🏔️ Jabal al-Nour & Cave Hira

The mountain holding the cave where the Prophet ﷺ used to retreat, and where the first revelation came to him. Perhaps the most significant site in Makkah after the Haram itself.

Distance from the Haram≈ 7 km
Climb to the cave1.5 – 2 hours up
DifficultyHard — steep, uneven, exposed
Typical taxiSAR 30–60 each way

Be realistic about the climb. It is a genuine mountain ascent of several hundred metres on rough steps, with little shade. People are taken ill on it every season, particularly in summer and particularly those who are older or unfit. There is no shame at all in viewing the mountain from the base — you lose nothing religiously by not reaching the cave.

If you do climb: go before Fajr or after Asr, carry two litres of water per person, wear real shoes, and turn back the moment you feel unwell.

🪨 Jabal Thawr

The cave where the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) hid for three nights during the Hijrah, while the Quraysh searched for them.

Distance from the Haram≈ 6 km
Climb1.5 – 2 hours up
DifficultyHard — harder than Hira, and the cave entrance is a tight squeeze
Typical taxiSAR 30–60 each way

Less visited than Jabal al-Nour and the climb is tougher. Most visitors view it from the road and reflect from there, which is entirely sufficient.

🪦 Jannat al-Mu'alla

Makkah's historic cemetery. Khadijah (may Allah be pleased with her), the Prophet's ﷺ first wife, is buried here, along with many of his family and early companions.

Distance from the Haram≈ 2–3 km
Time needed20–30 minutes
DifficultyEasy — walkable

Visit as you would any cemetery: give the greeting of peace to the people of the graves, reflect on death, make dua for the deceased. Do not make dua to them, do not touch or kiss the graves, and do not circle them. Visiting hours are limited and access for women varies — check locally.

🕌 Masjid Aisha (Taneem)

The nearest Miqat to Makkah, where Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) was instructed to enter Ihram. This is where people living in or staying in Makkah go to enter Ihram for an additional Umrah.

Distance from the Haram≈ 7–8 km
Time needed30–45 minutes
DifficultyEasy
Typical taxiSAR 25–50 each way

Has showers and changing facilities. Shared taxis run constantly between here and the Haram, so you rarely wait long.

Worth considering, though: many scholars hold that staying in the Haram to pray, recite and perform extra Tawaf is better than making repeated trips to Taneem for additional Umrahs. If your days are limited, that's a real choice to weigh.

🕌 Masjid al-Jinn

Marks the place where a group of jinn are reported to have listened to the Prophet ﷺ reciting the Quran and believed — the event referred to in Surah al-Jinn.

Distance from the Haram≈ 2 km
Time needed15–20 minutes
DifficultyEasy — walkable

A functioning mosque. Pray two rak'ah if you're there at the right time, and move on. Usually combined with Jannat al-Mu'alla, which is close by.

🏕️ Mina, Arafat & Muzdalifah

The three sites of Hajj. Outside the Hajj season they are quiet and open, and seeing them empty gives you a much clearer picture of what Hajj actually involves.

Mina≈ 8 km — the famous white tent city
Muzdalifah≈ 12 km — open ground between Mina and Arafat
Arafat≈ 22 km — Jabal al-Rahmah and the plain
Time for all three2–3 hours

Normally done as one loop by car. You can climb the small hill at Arafat — easy, maybe fifteen minutes. The pillar on top is a marker, nothing more: don't touch it for blessing or write on it.

🤝 Hudaybiyyah

Where the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah was signed and the Pledge of Ridwan was given beneath the tree. There is a mosque marking the site.

Distance from the Haram≈ 24 km
Time needed1–1.5 hours with travel
DifficultyEasy

Further out and less visited, so usually only included if you've hired a car for the day. Also functions as a Miqat for those coming from that direction.

🏛️ Makkah Museum & the Revelation Exhibition

Two worthwhile indoor options, and a sensible plan for the hottest part of the day. The Exhibition of the Two Holy Mosques Architecture holds original pieces — old doors of the Kaaba, sections of kiswah, the original minbar — and is genuinely remarkable.

Time needed1–2 hours
DifficultyEasy, air-conditioned

⚠️ Things to avoid at these sites

  • Touching, kissing or rubbing rocks, walls or pillars for blessing
  • Tying cloth or thread to anything, or leaving written notes in crevices
  • Praying facing a grave, a cave or a mountain
  • Asking the dead for anything, or asking them to intercede
  • Paying a "guide" to perform a special dua or ritual on your behalf
  • Taking soil, stones or water home as relics
  • Believing that visiting these places completes or improves your Umrah — it does not

You will see some of this happening. That it is common does not make it correct, and the authorities actively discourage it.
